# Enemies & Waves

Owner of: mob capabilities, flow-field pathfinding, stair-digging, climbable profiles, aggro, wave composition, roster.

`CharacterBody2D` per monster, one base script + per-type `EnemyStats` Resource: HP, speed, damage, XP, drops, sprites, plus **locomotion capabilities** — `move_class` (ground/fly), `jump_height` (0 = can't jump — the crawler, which climbs instead; 1–2 = walker/short-jumper; 4+ = big-jumper clearing low walls), `climb_speed` (0 = can't wall-climb), `is_biped` (gates climbable use), `dig_power`, `max_safe_fall` (tiles).

**Design principle: capabilities are speed, digging is correctness.** Every mob can dig, so no terrain state can ever soft-lock a mob; capabilities only make mobs faster. Mobs chew with an effective tool tier of 98 — everything but bedrock (99) breaks regardless of `min_tool_tier` gates, matching the flow field's only-bedrock-is-impassable cost model.

## Pathfinding — two shared dig-weighted flow fields
Computed **once per movement class**, not per mob:
- **Ground field.** Dijkstra outward from the Core, gravity-aware directional costs: *down* through air ≈ free (falling); *up/sideways* through air traversable only adjacent to a solid surface (wall-climbers) or through a climbable; solid tiles cost `1 + hardness/reference_dig_power`; deployables cost by HP.
- **Air field.** Flyers: air = 1, solids = chew cost (~20 lines atop the ground-field infra).

Restricted to the active wave region (**top 64 rows ≈ 12.8k cells**); one full synchronous recompute per trigger — terrain/deployable change, debounced ≤ 0.5 s (leading-edge, so it can't starve under continuous chewing). Solve cost is linear in cells, so the region depth is the cheapest lever on it: browser-measured **110 ms at 150 rows → 57 ms at 64 rows** (perf overlay, [ui.md](ui.md)). 64 covers the surface band (rows ~16–32) plus ~30 rows below, past `CAVE_MIN_ROW`; mobs deeper than that get no field and fall back to the direct-to-Core dig line, which always terminates. Second win from the same knob: edits below the region never arm the debounce, so deep player mining stops triggering recomputes entirely. The solve is **amortized across frames behind double-buffered arrays** (the pre-authorized fallback, now taken): `begin_recompute()` snapshots the terrain and seeds the back buffer, `step_recompute(budget_usec)` relaxes edges until the frame budget (4 ms) runs out, and the back buffer is swapped to the front only when complete — so a query never sees a half-solved field, and no single frame carries the whole solve. A change arriving mid-solve queues a rebuild rather than cancelling the one in flight (cancelling would leave the front buffer stale forever under continuous chewing). One exception stays synchronous: the flush at `wave_started`, because mobs must never spawn against a stale field, and that hitch lands on the frame already playing the wave banner — once per wave, not once per chew. **Accepted simplification:** fields use *reference* capabilities, so a mob may be routed toward a climbable it can't use — the dig fallback absorbs the error. No per-capability field variants. Because field guidance can also *cycle* for an under-capable mob (usable moves that loop at a route it can't take), a **stuck watchdog** backs this up: no net movement for ~1.5 s while trying to move switches the mob to the direct-to-Core dig line for ~6 s, which always terminates by chewing.

## Locomotion resolution (per mob, per frame)
Read the gradient at the current cell, resolve against capabilities in order: level/sideways → walk; up ≤ `jump_height` → jump; beyond → wall-climb if `climb_speed > 0`, or **CLIMB** a climbable if present and `is_biped` (3.5b — a real action beside WALK/JUMP/CHEW/FALL, actuated at `stats.speed`, *not* `climb_speed`, which is wall-climbing and belongs to 4.1's crawler); otherwise → **chew** the blocker via `Terrain.damage_tile` (same pipeline as the player, gated by `dig_power`). Blockers that are **entities** (Core, deployables) sit on air cells, so they take discrete melee hits (`damage` every `attack_cooldown` seconds) instead of the chew pipeline. A mob probes its own cell *and* the one ahead — the own-cell probe exists because the Core is 3×2 and a mob can stand inside it. ❗️**A `Deployable` in the mob's own cell is skipped, and only there** (3.1): a 1×1 torch is one the mob has already walked through, and stopping for it takes an early return that skips the stuck watchdog, starving the very thing a mob needs to escape a cycling route. The cell *ahead* otherwise keeps every deployable — mobs stopping to chew a lit tunnel's torches is the intended behaviour ([terrain.md](terrain.md) has the pacing numbers).

❗️**A second narrow exception (3.5b), beside the own-cell one: a climbable in the cell ahead is skipped too — nothing chews a ladder.** The entity probe runs *before* locomotion resolves, so without it a mob facing into a ladder returns it as an attackable entity, stops, and eats it: the CLIMB action is unreachable and the whole feature silently does nothing. ⚠️ **Direction-agnostic**, and deliberately so — the field routes a mob into a column from the *side*, so intent is horizontal on the frame it arrives and a vertical-only skip would let it chew rung 0 before ever climbing. And **not** gated on `is_biped` (§Climbables). The watchdog consequence is the mirror of the own-cell one: CLIMB counts as **movement** in the stuck watchdog, or a mob wedged on a ladder never arms the direct-dig fallback and hangs there for the rest of the wave. Climbing also resets the airborne apex, or descending ten rungs and stepping off bills ten tiles of fall damage.

**Fall handling:** falls beyond `max_safe_fall` damage mobs. When the gradient points down an unsafe drop, the mob **stair-digs**: chew the block under its own feet at the lip, descend one tile, re-measure, repeat until the remaining drop is safe — a shaft down the cliff face. (A literal diagonal stair is geometrically impossible: an unsafe drop ahead means the down-and-forward cell is already air.) Flyers/big-jumpers with high `max_safe_fall` skip it. Pit traps stay viable: hard-walled spike pits cost real chew time, and fall damage enables drop-trap designs.

## Climbables (ladder, rope, fireman pole)
Deployables with a **directional climb profile**: ladder = up/down full speed; rope = up slow / down normal; pole = **down only, fast**. Ground field treats climbable cells as cheap vertical edges respecting direction. Player ladders are **biped highways** during waves (remove pre-wave, hide behind hatches, or favor poles, which mobs can never ascend). Deliberate emergent texture.

**Shipped at 3.5b: the symmetric case only — the ladder**, for the player *and* for biped mobs. A ladder is up-and-down at full speed, which is precisely the profile that needs no profile mechanism, so the *directional* half (rope, pole, and whatever data structure expresses "down only") stays at 4.1 with the crawler's wall-climb. That is cut line ✂️8's pre-authorized shape ("Rope/pole climbables (**ladder only**)"), not a compromise. Everything a climbable *is* rides on one `is_climbable` export on the `Deployable` base ([automation.md](automation.md) §Categories → Utility owns the ladder itself and the stacking rule); at 4.1 the profile joins it beside that bool rather than replacing it.

- ❗️**`is_biped` gates ASCENDING, and nothing else.** It is read at exactly **one** site — the CLIMB branch in `EnemyLocomotion.decide` — and means "can ascend a climbable".
- ❗️**No mob chews a climbable**, biped or not (this **strikes** the earlier "biped-only" wording as a *chewing* rule — it was only ever about ascending). A non-biped that cannot go up a ladder goes **around** it, or — once 4.1's wall-climb lands — crawls **alongside** the column rather than eating it. Nothing observable changes at 3.5b, since the roster is walker-only and a walker is a biped; it is the crawler's behaviour that this decides in advance.
- **No soft-lock risk from the asymmetry.** The field is reference-capability (§Pathfinding), so it will happily route a crawler up a column it cannot use — the documented **accepted error**, absorbed by the dig fallback and backed by the stuck watchdog. Nothing new is needed for it.
- Field-side, an **ascending** move into a climbable cell is a `MOVE_COST` edge regardless of support. Only the **destination** need be climbable, which is what lets a mob step into the column from the cell below the bottom rung; the chain continues because every rung above is climbable too. ⚠️ A ladder still carries its `_entity_cost` HP surcharge — ~0.2 per rung at 20 HP, a fifth of one step. It mildly discourages ladder routes and blocks nothing: **leave it**, and do not add a `blocks_mobs := false` escape hatch pre-emptively.

## Aggro
Per-monster threat table `{node: threat}`; damage received adds threat to the attacker (player or turret). Highest threat above threshold overrides the Core target (direct local chase + chew, no field); threat decays so mobs resume pushing the Core. Turrets naturally tank. Tuning: threshold 8 (two swings of the starter pickaxe; a heavier weapon aggroes in one), linear decay 4/s (an ignored attacker is forgotten in a few seconds), swing range 1.5 tiles.

**Attack of opportunity (locked, 2.5):** on top of the threat table, a mob swings at the player standing inside its 1.5-tile reach, **no threat required** — checked before the Core push, so a player in the way is always the nearer problem. Without it a player who never attacks is invisible to the entire wave: the player is not in the terrain entity dict that `_attackable_entity` scans, and threat only ever comes from damage dealt, so a passive run would take zero damage and death would be opt-in. Touching a mob hurts too, but that half is the player's own hurtbox and belongs to [player-combat.md](player-combat.md); both paths deal `EnemyStats.damage` through the player's grace window, so they can't stack.

**On death (2.6):** a mob grants `EnemyStats.xp` and drops `drop_id × drop_count` as ordinary world pickups through the shared spawner ([player-combat.md](player-combat.md)) — the same Pickup a mined block produces, so mob loot feeds the looting XP channel with no second code path ([progression.md](progression.md)). Mob loot *content* is Day-4 work (4.2); today's authored drop is a placeholder that keeps the path exercised.

## Wave composition — reachability-adaptive
`Waves` reports "all spawned dead" by calling `Game.notify_wave_cleared()` (phase flow owned by [plan.md](../plan.md)). Budget `B(n) = 3 × 1.25^(n-1)`, floored, never below one mob (wave 1 = 3, wave 10 ≈ 22, wave 15 ≈ 68), spent on types unlocked by wave number — the composition table (`data/wave_roster.gd`) carries per-type **cost / unlock wave / pick weight**, and the whole wave is **pre-rolled into a spawn queue at wave start** from an RNG seeded on `(world_seed, wave_number)`: the count is then an exact head count for the HUD, the wave replays identically for a seed ([save.md](save.md)), and the Day-4 skew has exactly one hook. The queue then **trickles out one mob every 1.2 s, alternating buffer zones** ([world-gen.md](world-gen.md)) — spawn x is random in the 3–8 tiles nearest the *outer* world edge, standing on the buffer surface, so every mob crosses the full player-immutable band and no approach can be booby-trapped at its source. Above the ~25-alive cap the trickle stalls: the wave keeps its whole budget, the queue just drains slower. Accepted pacing consequence: a walker needs ~20 s to cross a buffer plus ~20 s of playable ground before first contact — mob `speed` and the trickle interval are the Day-4 levers if that reads as dead time. The wave manager reuses the ground field: at wave start, sample cost-to-Core at spawn cells vs. the untouched-terrain baseline (cached at world gen). This **fortification score** skews spawn weights toward **crawlers and flyers** when approaches are heavily sealed (even wave 1 in extreme cases); on open terrain flyers stay locked until mid waves. Tuning: the threshold must tolerate normal defenses (trigger around ratio > 3–4×); expose the score on the debug overlay for live calibration.

## Roster
Walker (baseline biped) · leaper (big-jumper) · digger (tanky, high dig power) · crawler (wall-climber + climbable user; counters trench bases) · flyer (mid-wave / fortification response) · spitter (stretch) · boss (stretch). Concurrent-spawn cap ~25 alive (perf).
